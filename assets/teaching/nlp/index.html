Course materials

A course on the modern aspects of Natural Language Processing. You will learn how today's NLP systems work end to end — from tokenization and language modelling to transformers and large language models, and how they are fine-tuned, retrieved from, aligned, served, and evaluated — and how to build, probe, and critically assess them.

Lectures are on Thursdays. Teaching team: J. Yang (responsible), A. Anand. Assessment: 20% individual assignments + 80% group project.

Assignments

Individual · auto-graded + short reflection · laptop-runnable (no GPU required)
A1

Tokenization & statistical LM

BPE, a bigram (add-k) LM, perplexity per-token vs per-byte, compared to a TULIP-served LLM (Gemma 4).

Group project briefs

GP Consultation Summaries (SOAP)

LLM summarisation + an LLM-as-judge benchmark + human-eval alignment.

flagship

Characterising Toxicity in LLMs

Generate → score → attribution → lexical/syntactic analysis.

Memorization: attack & defend LMs

Membership-inference & extraction attacks and defences on a small fine-tuned LM (Enron).

RAG for Open-Domain Complex QA

How context quality shapes RAG on multi-hop QA; hard-negative dense retrieval with ADORE (2WikiMultiHopQA).
