Word Embeddings

Turning words into dense vectors that capture meaning — by learning to predict the company they keep.

Recap

A lookup that just moves the question

Key idea

The token telescope has an ID; that ID picks one row of the embedding matrix E, and that row becomes the word's vector x. Lookup is trivial.

The real question is what the rows should contain. A random row says nothing about meaning. Today: what makes a row good, and how good rows are learned.

Recap

Sparse vectors that are blind to meaning

Two dead ends

One-hot: the dot product of any two different words is always 0 — every pair is orthogonal. planet is as unlike moon as it is unlike bill. It knows nothing about meaning.

Bag of words (sum of one-hots): forgets order (read our headline backwards → identical vector), and is as long as the vocabulary — 22 here, but 200,019 with the GPT-4o tokenizer, almost all zeros.

Question
If we never label anything, where could meaning possibly come from — using only raw text?
Firth, 1957: "You shall know a word by the company it keeps." What is a word's company?
Think — then press → to watch the reveal.
Recap

Meaning, for free, from context

The distributional hypothesis

A word's company = every word within 2 positions on either side. Count it: planet sits next to a twice, distant once, cold once — and moon has the same counts. bill keeps different company: new, climate, tax.

Use those count rows as vectors: cosine(planet, moon) = 1, cosine(planet, bill) = 0. Nobody said planets and moons are related — the vectors learned it from the company words keep.

Question
Counting the context table is itself vocabulary-sized and mostly zeros. How do we get short, dense vectors instead?
Idea: turn "company" into a prediction task. Predict what, from what — and how many classes?

Recap

Prediction turns text into training data

Key idea

Skip-gram: take a center word (water) and predict each word in its window — here 4 pairs: water→telescope, →spots, →on, →a. Slide the window over every sentence and raw text becomes labelled data, no annotation needed. CBOW is the mirror: from the surrounding words, predict the middle one.

The whole model is one big classifier: a word goes in, a context word comes out — but there is one class for every word in the vocabulary. A very large output.

The model, as one picture

One-hot → E → embedding → U → softmax

one-hot · E → v  →  scorec = uc · v  →  softmax over |V|
The softmax over the whole vocabulary P(c | w) = exp(uc · v)
∑w'∈V exp(uw' · v)
  • Loss = cross-entropy = −log P(real context word), e.g. −log P(spots | water).
  • Gradient w.r.t. the scores is, once again, p − y.

Recap

Two tables and a bottleneck

Key idea

one-hot × E simply selects one row = the word's embedding v (the narrow bottleneck). It fans back out: score v against every row of U, then softmax → distribution over all words. The real neighbour spots should top it.

The whole model is two tables: input embeddings E in, output embeddings U out, embedding in between. It is a massive multi-class classification — one class per word. The catch: that softmax denominator sums over the whole vocabulary.

Question
The model "learns" vectors — but exactly which parameters are stored, and what shape are they?
Every word appears both as a center word and as a context word. Does it get one vector, or two?

Recap

Two vectors per word

Method

Word2vec keeps two matrices, both |V| × d (here d = 2): E holds a word's vector as a center word, U as a context word. Look up water in E → vwater = (0.5, 0.2). Score of context word c = uc · vwater; a big dot product means a likely neighbour.

The catch: to compute the softmax denominator, every training pair touches all rows of U — and all of them get a gradient.

Question
The Google News model has ~3 million words — that is 3M dot products and exponentials for every pair. How do we make a single step cheap?
For the pair (water, spots), how much do the other 3 million words really matter?
Think — then press → to watch the reveal.
Recap

A yes/no question, k + 1 dot products

L = −log σ(uo · vc) − ∑k fakes log σ(−uk · vc)
Key idea

Instead of "which word out of millions?", ask "is this pair real or fake?" Keep only the real neighbour + k random fakes → just k + 1 scores instead of |V|.

−log σ(uo·vc) pushes the real pair together; the sum (note the minus inside, −uk·v) pushes the fakes apart. No sum over the vocabulary any more — that is where all the savings come from.

Question
Which words do we draw as fakes — and how many? If we drew uniformly, what would go wrong?
Words like the and of are everywhere. Should every negative be one of them?
Think — then press → to watch the reveal.
Recap

Flatten the frequencies, pick a small k

Method

Draw fakes by corpus frequency raised to the power 3/4. Frequent words lose a little (on: 0.115 → 0.093), rarer words gain. In real text a word 5,000× more frequent is only ~595× more likely to be drawn — so negatives aren't all the and of.

Our draw with k = 2: votes and tax — clearly not neighbours of water. How big is k? 5–20 for small datasets, 2–5 for large ones.

Worked example

One real pair, two fakes, by hand

  • Real pair (water, spots); fakes votes, tax. Four toy vectors, d = 2.
  • Forward per row: a dot product, then σ. Loss term = −log σ (real), −log(1−σ) (fakes).
  • Backward per score: gradient = σ − target — the same p − y from the softmax video.
Question
Run the forward pass on an untrained model. What do the three σ values look like — and which parameters will the update actually touch?
Think — then press → to watch the reveal.
Recap

θ ← θ − η · ∇, and only four rows move

Result

Forward: σ = 0.56, 0.5, 0.53 — an untrained model can't tell real from fake; loss = 2.05. Backward: grad = σ − target (for spots: 0.56 − 1 = −0.44), then chain rule gives each vector's update.

Update: θ ← θ − η·∇ with η = 0.5; spots moves toward water, votes/tax away → loss falls to 1.94. Only 4 rows changed: 1 of E + k+1 = 3 of U. The full softmax would have updated all 22 rows (millions, at scale).

Recap

Structure nobody put there by hand

Result

Over 600 epochs the loss falls 3.09 → 2.24 (it can't reach 0 — every word has several possible neighbours). Science words drift from politics words; words with the same company land together: planet–moon, water–ice, parliament–senate each at cosine +1; planet–bill point apart at −0.85.

These learned rows are the embeddings. The matrix E is nothing more than the first layer of the network, trained like any other weight.

Recap

From words, to vectors, to a decision

Method

At scale, directions carry meaning: king − man + woman lands near queen — a direction for "royal".

To classify our headline: look up each word's row of E, average the 8 vectors → x = (−0.89, −0.66), on the science side. Score each class, softmax → Science wins at 0.71. Training it is the softmax video again — cross-entropy, p − y — and that gradient can even flow back into E to fine-tune the embeddings.

Recap

Word embeddings

  • One-hot → everything orthogonal; bag of words → counts, but order-blind and huge.
  • Company gives meaning; word2vec learns it as the rows of E via a prediction task.
  • Negative sampling makes it cheap: one real pair vs k fakes, one small update at a time.
  • Average a sentence's vectors → feed the softmax classifier.
The limit → the bridge

These vectors are static: one vector per word, averaged over the whole corpus — so spots gets a single arrow whatever it means. A word's vector must instead depend on the words around it in this sentence.
